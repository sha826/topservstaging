The most common budgeting mistake in HVAC isn't spending too little — it's spending inconsistently. A big push when the schedule board looks empty, silence when it's full, and a budget that resets every time a slow week rattles someone. Here's the framework we use with heating and air companies instead.

## The short answer

**Growth-mode HVAC companies typically invest 5–10% of annual revenue in marketing.** Maintenance mode — a mature company defending its position — can run leaner, around 3–5%. Below 3%, you're not marketing; you're gambling on referrals and weather.

## What that means at your size

| Annual revenue | 5–10% budget | What it should buy |
|---|---|---|
| $1M | $50K–$100K/yr | Local SEO + Google Business Profile, LSA, review engine, a real website |
| $3M | $150K–$300K/yr | All of the above, plus PPC, brand video, email/reactivation, retargeting |
| $10M | $500K–$1M/yr | Full system: video-led brand, multi-channel paid, geofencing/OTT, recruiting marketing |

## Where the money should go

The allocation that holds up across most residential HVAC companies:

- **Foundation (always on):** website, local SEO, Google Business Profile, review generation. This is the compounding layer — it gets cheaper per lead every year it runs.
- **Demand capture (always on, throttled by season):** LSA and paid search for people looking for you *right now*.
- **Brand (the multiplier):** video, social, geofencing/OTT. This is what makes the capture layer cheaper — homeowners click the name they already recognize.
- **Reactivation (cheapest revenue you have):** email and SMS to past customers for maintenance plans and seasonal tune-ups.

Companies that cut the brand layer "because it's hard to measure" end up paying more per lead in the capture layer, forever, and calling it normal.

## Let seasonality bend the plan — not break it

HVAC demand is a sine wave; your budget shouldn't be a flat line. The pattern that works: hold the foundation steady year-round, scale demand-capture spend up ahead of the first heat wave and cold snap (not after — by then everyone's bidding), and use the shoulder seasons for brand video production, maintenance-plan pushes, and IAQ/duct campaigns that keep techs busy.

The mistake to avoid is going dark in the shoulders. The company that disappears in October is a stranger again by June — and pays stranger prices for leads.

## When to spend above 10%

Three situations justify it: entering a new market, launching a new service line (heat pumps have been this for many of our clients), or buying market share while a competitor stumbles. In all three, treat the overage as a time-boxed investment with a specific goal — not a new normal.

## FAQ

**Is 5–10% of revenue realistic for a company under $1M?**
The percentage holds, but the floor matters more: below roughly $50K/year it's hard to run even the foundation layer properly. Under that, concentrate everything on Google Business Profile, reviews, and LSA — the highest-intent, lowest-waste trio — and skip everything else until revenue supports it.
