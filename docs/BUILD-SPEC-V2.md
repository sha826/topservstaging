## 2. WHO THIS SITE IS FOR

The site is written for a specific buyer and designed to repel the rest. **That is intentional and it must survive review.**

### Built for

Established home service businesses with consistent demand and constrained growth. Owner led or leadership driven, willing to be visible on camera. Frustrated with ads, vendors and diminishing returns. Feeling price pressure despite doing quality work. Beginning to understand that brand creates leverage. Long term thinkers focused on enterprise value, not quick wins.

### Not for

Buyers looking for cheap or fast lead generation. Set it and forget it mindsets. Owners unwilling to be visible. Businesses that see marketing strictly as a cost. Anyone expecting short term tactics to fix structural problems. Commercial, multifamily, developer or general contractor buyers, because BrandFormance markets residential home service demand and does not translate.

**It is acceptable and expected for the wrong buyer to feel disqualified. That is the site working as designed. Do not soften it in review.**

### Revenue guidance

Roughly $1M to $10M annual revenue, framed as guidance rather than a gate. We have worked successfully above and below, and JB says so on camera. **The assessment does the actual qualifying.**

### Programs page 4: Pricing

> **This section is rewritten in v2.0. Do not build from the v1.0 price table.**
>
> v1.0 published three programs at fixed weekly rates with annual totals. **We no longer publish a price table.** Price is derived per client by the Growth Engine from actual scope, at target margin, and no two clients in the same market price the same.

**What the page publishes:**

| Element | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| The floor | Programs start at $1,000 per week. That is the floor rate to manage the work. It is a floor, not a menu price. |
| The activation | A one-time $10,000 activation in month one, covering the two day video shoot, travel, and the first month of build. |
| What sets the number | Market size, competitive saturation, current brand position, service area, and video scope. Stated plainly so the range is understood without a figure attached. |
| How the number is reached | The assessment and the research modules produce the scope. The scope produces the price. The program is assigned by diagnosis, not chosen from a menu. |
| What it replaces | Position against low cost agencies without attacking them. This replaces multiple vendors and internal hires. |
| When it is not a fit | Say it plainly. This page should disqualify price shoppers, tactical buyers and short term thinkers. |

**Why no table.** Publishing three fixed rates invites a prospect to self-select a tier before we have diagnosed anything, which is the exact behaviour the diagnosis model exists to prevent. It also fixes a price against a scope we have not seen. The floor plus the activation gives an honest anchor without either problem.

#### The 48 and 52 week rule, stated once so nobody publishes the wrong annual

> **Both numbers are correct and they describe different things.**
>
> **Year one is 48 weekly payments.** Month one is the activation. Weekly billing starts month two. Year one is the activation plus 48 weeks.
>
> **Steady state is 52 weekly payments.** Every year after the first bills 52 weeks.
>
> **Never publish either annual figure on the site.** If a prospect asks on a call, give the real number for their program and be explicit about which year they are asking about.
>
> **Never quote a monthly figure anywhere.** Not on the site, in a deck, a proposal, an email or a call. A monthly number invites a prospect to multiply by twelve and be wrong in both directions.

How it is said. Correct: a thousand a week, plus a one-time ten thousand to get started. Wrong: four thousand a month. Wrong: about fifty two thousand a year.

## 5. THE BRAND ASSESSMENT AND THE BRAND SCORE

> **Rewritten in v2.0. These are two objects with two names and they must never be conflated.**
>
> v1.0 put a public Brand Score on the site as the primary conversion asset, returning a number out of 100, and required that the site number and the sales console number match. **That requirement cannot be met and should not be attempted.**

### 5.1 Why the number cannot go public

A public version runs on less data. No client input, no revenue, no division context, no confirmed service area. It will produce a different number than the console, and **the first prospect who scores 61 on the site and 43 on the call stops trusting both numbers.** A grade is robust to thinner data. A number is not.

The six components, their weights and the normalization rule are also the method. **Normalizing against the local competitive set rather than an absolute threshold is the asset**, because 100 reviews means one thing against a field of 400 and another against 2,800. Publishing the score publishes that.

### 5.2 The Brand Assessment, public

Lives at /brand-assessment. Returns **a grade, not a number.** The four grades are the four levels of brand equity in JB’s book, named exactly as he names them there, so **nothing proprietary leaves the building.** Use his labels verbatim. A grade the site invents is a grade we have to defend.

| Grade | What the visitor is told |
| --- | --- |
| No Brand Equity | Effectively invisible. Prospects do not recognize the name and have no reason to trust it, so they pick at random from a list. Statistically closer to zero than to one hundred. |
| Name Recognition | Prospects recognize the name but do not care about it. Familiarity without preference, so they still search generically when something breaks. |
| Household Name | Searched for by name. The customer skips the category search entirely. Leads cost a fraction of what everyone else pays. |
| Negative Brand Equity | Actively chosen against. Fix the operation before spending another dollar on marketing, because marketing only accelerates what is already happening. |

Each grade returns a short explanation of what it means for their business and a booking. **That is the primary conversion asset and the primary tracked metric.** A grade with a consequence converts harder than a number without context.

#### Optional inputs

The visitor may optionally supply revenue, service area, primary trade and whether they run a membership program. **Optional, never gated.** The assessment returns a grade either way. Supplied inputs sharpen the grade and pre-populate the console, which shortens the strategy call.

#### What the public assessment never shows

The numeric score. The six component names. The weights. The normalization method. Any component level breakdown. **If it is not in the book, it does not appear on the site.**

### 5.3 The Brand Score, internal

Lives in the sales console and the client data object only. Six components, weighted. **Canonical. The site must not invent its own definition and no other document may restate these weights.**

| Component | Weight | How it is computed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website Strength | 25 | API computed. Speed, structure, conversion paths, schema. |
| Social Media | 25 | AI assessed. Presence, cadence, whether the owner is on camera. |
| Online Reputation | 20 | API computed. Review volume, rating, velocity, response behaviour. |
| Brand Visibility | 15 | API computed. Branded search volume and local pack position. |
| Digital Consistency | 10 | API computed. Name, address, phone and claim consistency. |
| Market Positioning | 5 | AI assessed. Does the messaging survive the swap test. |

#### Scoring rules

**Normalize against the local competitive set, never an absolute threshold.** Score per market where a company serves more than one, since reviews and profile attach to the home market and do not transfer while website and consistency transfer fully. **Grade the division being marketed, not the company.** A plumbing company with seventy nine years of equity can be unknown in HVAC.

#### One engine, two output modes

Alex builds one scoring engine. **Public mode returns a grade. Internal mode returns the score, the components and the grade.** Not two engines. The grade boundaries are the same in both modes, which is why a prospect who lands on Name Recognition online lands on Name Recognition in the console.

This also removes a dependency. brandscore.biz custody is unresolved. **A grade-only assessment lives on topservdigital.com and does not need that domain.**

## 6. BRAND FREQUENCY, CORRECTED

> **v1.0 published brand frequency as 3, 4 and 5 times per week by program. That is wrong and it contradicts the doctrine the whole company is built on.**
>
> **The frequency floor is 3 times weekly and it is fixed at every rung.** Frequency is never the variable. **Geography is.** When a budget will not carry the map at the frequency floor, the map shrinks. The frequency does not.
>
> Below the coverage threshold the compounding effect does not form at all, which is why frequency cannot be traded away. What scales with program is how much geography we can hold, not how often we show up in it.

Anywhere the site describes what a larger program buys, it says **more geography held at the same frequency**, never more frequency. This is a differentiator worth stating plainly, because every competitor sells more impressions and we sell more territory owned properly.

## 11. CLIENT CONTENT PERMISSIONS

New in v2.0, from Alejandro. This is the model for what a client can edit on their own site once we build it for them. It is specified here because **the same model runs on topservdigital.com first**, which makes our own rebuild the working prototype rather than a separate job.

> **The project update feature is the zone photo pipeline. Build it at that priority.**
>
> Client submitted project updates carry photos, a description, a location and the services performed. That is the input the Local Dominance Engine is starving for. **A zone page cannot publish without at least three photos shot inside that zone**, and without this pipeline the automated half produces a page indistinguishable from every other agency city page while reporting green on its own tasks.
>
> This is not a nice-to-have CMS feature. It is a blocking dependency for every Zone Campaign.

### What clients can add and manage

| Content type | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Media room updates | Press releases, announcements, news, images, publish date, optional downloadable documents |
| Project updates | Photos, short description, location resolved to a zone, services performed, service areas. Once approved these appear on the relevant service area and project pages. |
| Team updates | Staff photos, bios, roles, certifications, new hires |
| Testimonials | Site testimonials only. Not Google reviews. See below. Approval step before publishing. |
| Business information | Email, hours, social links, holiday closures, emergency notices. Phone and address excluded, see below. |
| Gallery and proof | Gallery images, case studies, awards, partner logos, event announcements |
| Homepage announcements | Preset layouts, expiry dates required |

### What clients can never change

SEO titles, meta descriptions, schema, internal linking, URLs, redirects, canonicals, sitemap or robots settings. Core service page copy, location page copy, homepage positioning, or any SEO optimized content. Navigation, page hierarchy, templates, header and footer, branding system, fonts, colours or layout. Conversion elements including primary calls to action, forms, tracking, booking flows, phone number tracking or lead routing. Analytics, pixels, tag manager, integrations, CRM connections, automations, user permissions or site settings. Legal pages, accessibility, security, cookie controls or anything touching hosting and domain configuration. **Publishing an entirely new page without internal review.**

### Three corrections to the model as proposed

**1. Phone and address are not client editable.** NAP consistency is a blocking gate in the delivery engine. Identity contradictions cap the entire local pack grid, because uncertainty about who a business is never gets a top three slot anywhere. A client changing their phone on the site and not on the profile and forty directories has capped their own grid and we would find out on a re-scan three months later. Either deny both, or gate them behind a propagation workflow that updates every surface at once. Hours, email, holiday closures and emergency notices stay open.

**2. Location resolves to a zone, not free text.** A technician typing a city name ties to nothing. The field resolves to a zone identifier against the Zone object so photos increment the zone photo count. **Free text gives us photos we cannot count, which is the same as not having them.**

**3. Testimonials are not reviews.** A site testimonial is content we control. A Google review is not, cannot be edited or ported, and a real review naming the zone city is what closes a zone. **Two objects. They must not share a name in the schema** or someone closes a zone that is not closed.

### The AI experience

The client says things like add this press release to the media room, create a project update for this roofing job in Miami, or update our holiday hours. **The system works within predefined content types and templates, previews the result, flags missing details, and requires approval before anything goes live.**

**Approval routing: client submits, TopServ reviews, TopServ publishes.** Same shape as the artifact lifecycle in the Growth Engine, and the only version where the SEO layer stays intact.

**Expiry is required on anything temporal.** Promotional banners and emergency notices both, or we end up with a hurricane closure banner still live in March.
